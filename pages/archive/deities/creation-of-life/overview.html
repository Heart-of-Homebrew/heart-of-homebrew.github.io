Creation of Life

The Creation of Life is the deific power that grants the ability to form bodies capable of housing spirits and becoming souls. Through it, flesh is patterned, vessels are prepared, and entire peoples may be called into being.

The Power to Shape Vessels

Where other powers govern motion, time, or distance, this power concerns the architecture of living bodies themselves. It refines inert matter into structures that can receive a greater spirit, bind it into a greater soul, and, in rare cases, sustain the making of a deific soul.

The bearer of this power does not create spirits directly, but fashions vessels fit to hold them. In its gentler uses, it shapes ordinary mortal lineages. In its grander expressions, it forges bodies capable of bearing deific presence without being wholly undone by it.

Peoples Born for Other Deities

In the oldest records, the bearer of Creation of Life is called upon not only to seed their own followers, but to prepare entire peoples on behalf of other deities. Lineages are tuned toward certain callings, climates, or covenants, so that when another power lays claim to them, their bodies and souls are already shaped to answer.

Some nations trace their origin to such covenants, forged when a deity requested a people fit for their patronage and another wielded this power to craft the first bodies of that kind. The details of those arrangements — what was promised, what was owed, and what was broken — remain topics of heated dispute among later scholars.

Known Bearers & Succession

Tradition holds that Ceri first bore the Creation of Life and used it to pattern the earliest mortal vessels. In later eras, the power is said to be shared by Olruc Ceri and Senek Ceri, each emphasizing different strains of its potential — one toward resilience and endurance, the other toward fecundity and refinement.

This article will eventually catalogue known acts of creation, the peoples shaped by them, and the succession of bearers who have carried this power across ages.
